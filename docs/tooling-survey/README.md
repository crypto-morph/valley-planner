# Valley Planner owns the plan; borrow everything else

No maintained vanilla, save-driven tool does what Valley Planner's core does. Nobody else builds a dated Community Center critical path to a deadline, checks a to-do against the next morning's save, or reads your inventory to say ship, process or hold. The community covers almost everything around that core, and usually covers it better. MouseyPounds' Stardew Predictor (MIT) forecasts Traveling Cart stock, rain days, night events and geodes from the save. stardew.app (MIT) and Stardew Checkup (MIT) dominate perfection tracking. Thorinair's Stardew-Profits (MIT) is the maintained 1.6 crop and artisan profit oracle. Pathoschild's StardewMods (MIT) encode gift tastes, fish spawn rules, bundles and crop water status from 1.6 data. The practical strategy follows from that. Keep and deepen the planning features, which have no rival. Turn the probabilistic Traveling Cart and rain odds into deterministic forecasts, by clean-room reimplementation checked against MIT test fixtures. Switch game-data extraction from xnbcli to StardewXnbHack. Use MIT tools as test oracles rather than runtime dependencies. Link out for layouts, full perfection tracking, fish ponds and bite-probability maths. Two housekeeping items came up while checking the repo: it currently commits two raw unpacked game files, and it has no LICENSE file. Fix both before any public release.

## Players install information by the million and plans by the thousand

The SMAPI mod numbers show what players actually want. In-game information mods have huge reach: NPC Map Locations has about **14.6M downloads**, Lookup Anything about **10.0M**, and CJB Show Item Sell Price and UI Info Suite 2 about **2.7M each** ([Nexus: NPC Map Locations](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/239), [Nexus: Lookup Anything](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/541)). Planning and to-do mods get one to two orders of magnitude fewer. To-Dew, a manual list, has about **1.08M**, Deluxe Journal about **177K**, and Automatic To-Do List about **35K** ([Nexus: To-Dew](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/7409), [Nexus: Deluxe Journal](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/11436), [Nexus: Automatic To-Do List](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/26008)). Nexus does not say whether these are unique or total downloads, so read them as relative. Even so, the gap is wide enough to set priorities. Players want quick answers when they need them, so Ask search and a short, automatic Morning summary matter more than any further planning surface.

Planning is where the open field is. The newest in-game planner, Farmer's Notebook, comes closest. It has Today, a three-day Upcoming view, and crops ranked into tiers by whether they mature before season end ([Nexus: Farmer's Notebook](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/48121)). It still has no deadline-ranked Community Center path. The only Community Center deadline tool on the web is Stardew Valley Sprinkler's Deadline Planner, which gives planting cutoffs, fishing windows and building lead times. It is closed source, "All rights reserved", and you enter your situation by hand rather than it reading your save ([Sprinkler tools](https://stardewvalleysprinkler.com/tools)). The save-readers are explicitly descriptive. Checkup reports "what is missing" ([MouseyPounds/stardew-checkup](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-checkup)). stardewvalleyids.com's Checkup is read-only ([stardewvalleyids.com](https://stardewvalleyids.com/checkup)). Valley Ledger states that its calculator is "not a hidden recommendation" ([stardewplanner.xyz](https://stardewplanner.xyz/)). Every web crop optimiser takes level, fertilizer and gold as typed input; the closest is mschult2's scheduler, which also asks for gold and tiles by hand ([mschult2/stardew-planner](https://github.com/mschult2/stardew-planner)). Three features exist only inside SMAPI: auto-generated daily to-dos, unwatered-crop warnings and "what next" suggestions from an LLM agent ([Nexus: Daily Tasks Report Plus](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/20871), [stardew-together](https://github.com/Zsh123456-BOOP/stardew-together)). A player who refuses mods has no way to get them except Valley Planner. Small private or non-English tools may exist that the survey missed, so "no rival" means "none found".

## Feature-by-feature: keep the planner, borrow the reference layer

"Add" means a gap that another tool fills and that fits a once-a-day save reader. "Link out" means another tool does it better and rebuilding it would not be worth the effort. "Reuse" names code or data we can lawfully read, port or test against, with its licence. MIT and BSD code can be ported with the copyright notice kept. GPL and MPL code can be read, but copying it brings copyleft obligations. Unlicensed or all-rights-reserved code is for ideas only.

The table holds a few judgement calls. Bundles stays a "keep" even though checklists are everywhere, because the critical path needs bundle state as its input. Its value is in feeding the path, not in being one more tracker. The Fish finder stops short of full bite-probability maths, because Fishing Info Overlays gets near-100% accuracy only by repeatedly simulating `getFish` at the player's live tile, which a once-a-day save reader cannot reproduce ([Nexus: Fishing Info Overlays](https://www.nexusmods.com/stardewvalley/mods/8970)). Spoiler control comes up in two places. Lookup Anything's progression mode hides gift tastes and puzzle solutions until they are learned in-game, and Checkup ships spoiler warnings ([Lookup Anything README](https://github.com/Pathoschild/StardewMods/blob/develop/LookupAnything/docs/README.md), [Checkup](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-checkup)). For a planner that tells the player what to do, an optional spoiler-light mode is worth a small amount of effort.

## Cart stock and rain odds can become forecasts

The biggest single improvement on offer is replacing the critical path's Traveling Cart and weather odds with exact predictions. On PC and mobile 1.6, the cart's base stock is a pure function of the save's game ID, the day number and the legacy-RNG flag. That covers 10 random objects with their prices and quantities, plus the furniture item and the seasonal special. The base RNG is seeded from **(daysPlayed, uniqueIDForThisGame/2)**, through xxHash32 by default and through an additive sum when `useLegacyRandom` is set. Each 1.6 extra (Rarecrow, Coffee Bean, skill book and so on) uses its own "synced" RNG keyed on a hashed string ([stardew-predictor.js](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-predictor/blob/master/stardew-predictor.js)). Rabbit's Foot (object 446) sits inside the random-object id range, so it would come out of the base draws. That is inferred from the range; the per-item exclusion list was not checked. The Year-1 Red Cabbage guarantee can be predicted forward from the save's `visitsUntilY1Guarantee` counter by counting cart visits: Fridays, Sundays and Night Market days 99–101 ([stardew-predictor.js](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-predictor/blob/master/stardew-predictor.js)). A Red Cabbage odds model built on luck therefore becomes a dated fact: the cabbage arrives on visit N.

Weather is only partly deterministic. Spring and fall rain comes from a synced RNG keyed on "location_weather" with a **0.183** threshold. Summer rain rises from 0.12 by 0.003 per day, days 13 and 26 are fixed storms, and the Green Rain day is drawn from {5, 6, 7, 14, 15, 16, 18, 23}. The predictor deliberately reports only wet versus dry: "Rain could become Storm", "Sun could become Wind or Snow", and winter is not predicted at all ([stardew-predictor.js](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-predictor/blob/master/stardew-predictor.js)). A rain totem overrides the forecast. The planner should therefore show rain/no-rain as certain for spring through fall, keep storms (apart from the fixed days) and winter as probabilities, and drop the forecast after any totem use. The two reference implementations disagree on the Green Rain seed. MouseyPounds uses (year×777, gameID) and CuiYinYin uses (777, gameID), so they agree only in year 1 ([WeatherPredictor.cs](https://github.com/CuiYinYin2023/StardewSeedSearcher/blob/main/StardewSeedSearcher/Features/WeatherPredictor.cs)). That is exactly the kind of question the house rule of checking against decompiled 1.6 settles. Read the game method locally, then encode the behaviour in our own words.

A clean-room implementation is small. It needs three published algorithms: .NET `System.Random` (the Knuth subtractive generator from Microsoft's reference source), xxHash32 over little-endian int32 arrays, and xxHash32 over UTF-8 strings ([cs-random.js](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-predictor/blob/master/cs-random.js)). On top of those sits a per-feature seed recipe, verified against decompiled `Utility.CreateRandomSeed`, `GetDeterministicHashCode` and the cart and weather methods the predictor's comments name. The best ready-made oracle is skyswordw's StardewSeedSearcher-Web (MIT). It states support for 1.6.15 on PC, Android and iOS, and ships fixture and parity tests ([GitHub](https://github.com/skyswordw/StardewSeedSearcher-Web)). The limits must be shown to the user. Switch uses a different RNG, so predictions should be labelled "PC/mobile only" ([Oshawk/stardew-seed-cracker](https://github.com/Oshawk/stardew-seed-cracker)). The cart's item pool changes between patches; the predictor needed an update for 1.6.3 ([README](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-predictor)). One February 2026 forum report of a wrong cart prediction on 1.6 remains unexplained ([forum thread](https://forums.stardewvalley.net/threads/web-apps-stardew-checkup-predictor-and-fair-helper.28393/latest)). The Morning summary should keep a "verify at the cart" note until our own next-morning checks have confirmed a few forecasts. Geodes and mystery boxes depend on per-player counts and multiplayer IDs rather than on dates, and mine floors drift within a session. Those are better left to a link to the Predictor.

## StardewXnbHack and MIT oracles beat runtime dependencies

The data pipeline should change tools. The repo's `.gitignore` says the unpacker in use is xnbcli. The official wiki says xnbcli "can't unpack XNB files containing data models (like Data\FishPondData or Data\Movies); use StardewXnbHack if you need those" ([Wiki: Editing XNB files](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Modding:Editing_XNB_files)). xnbcli's last release was in January 2021, and its licence metadata is contradictory: GitHub says GPL-3.0, the README says LGPL-3.0 ([LeonBlade/xnbcli](https://github.com/LeonBlade/xnbcli)). StardewXnbHack is MIT and written by Pathoschild. It runs from the user's own game folder and writes every `Data/*` asset as JSON ([Pathoschild/StardewXnbHack](https://github.com/Pathoschild/StardewXnbHack)). Its catch is that SMAPI must be installed **for the extraction step only**. That does not break "vanilla for the player", because nothing runs at play time, but the README should say so plainly. Its latest release is 1.1.2 from November 2024, and whether it handles 1.6.15+ cleanly has not been confirmed. stardew.app proves the pattern works in production: maintainers drop StardewXnbHack's `Data/` output into `scripts/content/`, and Python scripts build derived JSON that is committed to the repo ([stardew.app scripts/content](https://github.com/communitycenter/stardew.app/tree/main/scripts/content)).

For rules, the most valuable reuse is as test oracles, not dependencies. No maintained, versioned 1.6 save-parsing library exists in Python, Rust or C#; the mature parsers live inside apps ([MouseyPounds/stardew-checkup](https://github.com/MouseyPounds/stardew-checkup), [chiefpansancolt/stardew-valley-data](https://github.com/chiefpansancolt/stardew-valley-data)). Keeping our own tolerant XML reader, gated on the save's `gameVersion` as Checkup and Predictor do, is the right call. The oracles worth wiring into tests are these:

| Oracle | What to test | Licence |
|---|---|---|
| Stardew-Profits `data.js` and formulas | Crop profit, artisan outputs and the changelog's pitfalls: Speed-Gro rounding (v1.7.5), Hops making Pale Ale (v1.7.2), Artisan bonus on Casks/Kegs/Jars (v1.6.11), Broccoli regrow (v1.6.9), Powdermelon price (v1.7.3) ([changes.html](https://github.com/Thorinair/Stardew-Profits/blob/master/changes.html)) | MIT |
| StardewSeedSearcher-Web fixtures | Cart and weather seeding parity | MIT |
| MouseyPounds Predictor | Cart, night events, weather; `?id=&dp=` URLs for link-outs | MIT |
| Checkup and stardew.app parsers | Bundle, shipping, museum and friendship field paths in the save | MIT |
| Pathoschild/StardewMods | Gift-taste precedence, fish spawn rules, remixed bundles, crop water and harvest, machine recipes ([source tree](https://github.com/Pathoschild/StardewMods/tree/develop/LookupAnything)) | MIT |
| brokencygnus fishing calc | Bite odds, read for understanding only | GPL-3.0 |

Mod code is a guide to which game method to mirror, not drop-in logic. Lookup Anything and Data Layers call the game's own methods at runtime, so porting them still means reimplementing what those calls do. Lookup Anything also warns that some special drops "are hardcoded and can't be detected" from data files ([README](https://github.com/Pathoschild/StardewMods/blob/develop/LookupAnything/docs/README.md)), so checking against decompiled 1.6 remains essential.

## Facts and your own code are safe; game files and decompiled code are not

ConcernedApe has published no fan-content policy. His Terms of Use cover only his websites. They assert the "Stardew Valley®" and "ConcernedApe™" trademarks and forbid linking in a way that suggests endorsement ([stardewvalley.net/terms](https://www.stardewvalley.net/terms/)). The only documented enforcement is a pair of July 2016 GitHub DMCA notices against repositories of **decompiled source code** ([github/dmca 2016](https://github.com/github/dmca/blob/master/2016/2016-07-11-StardewValley.md)). Meanwhile, derived-JSON projects such as stardew.app have run under MIT for years without known action. Community practice, which is not law, ranks the options from safe to risky. Our own code that reads the user's save and locally extracted data is safest. Fact-level derived JSON (ids, prices, growth days) comes next. Wiki text is allowed only under its **CC BY-NC-SA 3.0** terms, kept in a separate, attributed file ([Wiki: Copyrights](https://stardewvalleywiki.com/Stardew_Valley_Wiki:Copyrights), [save editor LICENSE.md](https://github.com/colecrouter/stardew-save-editor/blob/main/LICENSE.md)). Sprites and verbatim game strings rest on weaker fair-use claims. Redistributing raw unpacked files or decompiled C# is clearly off-limits.

For Valley Planner, that means four things. Decompiled 1.6 may be read locally to check rules, but never committed or quoted. Behaviour should be described in our own code, as the Predictor does. Item names should be shown from the user's extracted `Strings`/`Data` at runtime, not from a committed dump. The project should link to the wiki rather than copy it, and should call itself "a planner for Stardew Valley", with a not-affiliated disclaimer and no logo. Ported MIT snippets keep their notices. GPL (brokencygnus, Gift Taste Helper, ScheduleViewer) and MPL (Perfection Handbook) code is read-only unless the project chooses a compatible licence. UI Info Suite 2 (no licence), KhloeLeclair's mods ("All rights reserved"), Underscore76's SeedFinding (no licence), selbysaurus and Sprinkler are for ideas and links only. None of these lines has been tested in court, and UK fair dealing is narrower than US fair use. Keeping the repo to our own code plus fact-level data is the posture that doesn't depend on either.

## Conclusion

The survey changes the project's identity more than its feature list. Valley Planner should not try to be the reference layer of Stardew facts and checklists. Pathoschild, MouseyPounds and stardew.app already maintain that layer under MIT, and it is better used as a test oracle and link target. What no one else does is the decision layer: given this save and this deadline, what to do today, verified tomorrow. Every recommendation above either sharpens that layer or removes a source of uncertainty under it. Deterministic cart and rain forecasts are the clearest case. They turn "a 70% chance the cabbage shows up in time" into "it arrives on Sunday the 14th".

Determinism also changes the to-do's next-morning check. Once forecasts are exact, each morning's save also tests the planner's own model. A cart or weather mismatch becomes a bug report against our reimplementation rather than bad luck. That makes the project's habit of checking rules against decompiled 1.6 into a running check in production, which none of the tools surveyed have.
